## 4 Results

### 4.1 Thematic map
**A. Alternative sequence mixers (LIT05-001…039).** Lineage: linear attention (001) → Hyena/RWKV/RetNet/xLSTM (002, 005–007) → selective SSMs Mamba/Mamba-2 (003, 004) → delta-rule family DeltaNet / Gated DeltaNet / RWKV-7 / Kimi Delta Attention / GDN-2 (022–024, 026, 037) → test-time-learning memories (031 TTT, 032 Titans, 034 MesaNet, 035, 036). Consistent theme across independent sources: fixed-size state ⇒ weaker exact recall/copying (008, 013, 015, 018), partly mitigated by delta-rule updates (022), read-twice tricks (017) and by keeping a minority of attention layers (009, 025, 010–012). Mechanism-level work (018) says retrieval in both Transformers and SSMs runs through a few Gather-and-Aggregate heads, which motivates cheap Transformer→hybrid distillation (020, 021, 145). Industry evidence conflicts: Kimi Linear (026) reports a hybrid beating full attention at 3B-active/48B under identical recipe, whereas MiniMax (152) reports reverting to full attention for M2 after multi-hop-reasoning deficits at scale and infrastructure problems (no numbers). Mamba-3 (029) is challenged on edge latency by 030.

**B. Recurrent depth / latent reasoning / non-AR (LIT05-040…068).** Looped/recurrent-depth models (040–042, 054–056, 148) claim parameter-efficient reasoning; tiny recursive reasoners HRM (045) and TRM (046) claim large ARC/Sudoku gains from ~27M/7M params. Independent analyses substantially deflate the architectural story for HRM: ARC Prize ablations (150) attribute most of the performance to the outer refinement loop and task augmentation with a near-parity vanilla Transformer; mechanistic papers (047, 048) find fixed-point violations, multiple fixed points, no clean constraint features; a replication-style study of nested recursion (049) is null but self-declared under-powered; UT+ACT work (050) shows ACT training fragility. Diffusion LMs: two preprints agree that masked diffusion beats AR when unique data is scarce and epochs are many (062, 063); LLaDA (058) is competitive with LLaMA3-8B but against self-built AR baselines. KAN (066) vs controlled MLP comparison (067) is the clearest "novel architecture deflated by matched comparison" case.

**C. Efficient training/inference (LIT05-069…105).** Headline claims: BitNet b1.58 (069, 070), Muon ~2× over AdamW (080), speculative decoding / EAGLE / Medusa (083–085), MLA/NSA (088, 089), MoE scaling (094, 095). Independent controls: unequal-tuning critique of optimizer speedups (081; also 082), ternary export-pipeline audit (071), low-bit hurts well-trained small models more (075), KV-compression pitfalls and benchmarks (092, 093), Chinchilla replication (097), model-collapse counter-evidence (103), speculative-decoding benchmark (086).

**D. Self-learning / reflection / self-improvement (LIT05-106…143).** Prompt-level reflection (Self-Refine 106, Reflexion 107) is contradicted for *intrinsic* reasoning self-correction by 108/109. Training-level self-improvement (STaR 110, self-reward 111, SEAL 114, Absolute Zero 115, DGM 117) is supported by the quantitative "generation-verification gap" analysis (112) and the sharpening theory (113: self-improvement cannot add information absent from the model — summary-level only). RLVR critiques (125–127) show that gains can be explained by base-model priors or contamination; reasoning-evaluation disputes (133–137) show benchmark-design confounds. Introspection (129, 130) and faithfulness (131, 132) results are only metadata/abstract-level. Continual-learning/plasticity (138–141) is the main bridge to "learn from scratch in any environment".

### 4.3 Contested claims (preserve both sides)
1. **Hybrid linear attention beats/ matches full attention at scale.** For: 026, 025, 009. Against/unquantified: 152 (MiniMax: multi-hop reasoning deficits, infrastructure), 008 (copying limits). Cannot adjudicate: MiniMax gives no model sizes/scores; Kimi compares inside one lab; the two blogs/preprints use different models and data.
2. **HRM's brain-inspired hierarchy drives performance.** For: 045. Against: 150 (ablations, partial read), 047/048 (mechanistic, abstract-level + partial). Unresolved for TRM (046): same confounds may apply — not tested.
3. **BitNet-style ternary models are "as good as FP16 at same size/tokens".** For: 069, 070. Against/qualifying: 071 (export-pipeline fidelity), 075 (low-bit penalty depends on training level), 077 (unified comparison purports to settle 1.58 vs 4-bit; not read).
4. **Muon ≈ 2× AdamW.** For: 080 (authors). Against: 081 (rigorous tuning, ≤1.2B scale). Both could hold: different scale, data regime and tuning protocol.
5. **"Reasoning collapse" in reasoning models.** 133 vs 134/135/136/137: design confounds (token limits, unsolvable instances); 134 self-describes experiments as preliminary.
6. **Model collapse is inevitable under self-training.** 102 vs 103 (accumulating real+synthetic data avoids collapse in tested settings).
7. **Intrinsic self-correction helps.** 106 vs 108/109. External-signal setups behave differently (not re-tested here).

### 4.4 Open problems (as supported by the sources, not new claims)
- No independent large-scale (≥7B) replication of any hybrid-vs-attention or SSM-vs-Transformer result found; evidence is single-lab or small-scale.
- Verification gap: no standard protocol for matched-compute, matched-tuning architecture comparison; optimizer and architecture claims both shrink under tuned baselines (081, 067, 150).
- Interpretability of non-Transformer architectures is nascent (018, 047, 048, 148); compatibility of SAE/transcoder tools with recurrent states is unaddressed in the sources read.
- Self-improvement limits: formal ceiling (113, 112) vs empirical zero-data claims (115) vs base-model dependence (125, 126); contamination (127) not systematically controlled.
- Continual learning / plasticity for from-scratch learners is mostly studied at small scale (140), with 2026 LLM-scale work (138, 139) abstract-level only.

## 8 Next actions — small CPU experiments (each ≤30 CPU-min on 2 vCPU/8 GiB, PyTorch CPU; preregister before running; one model process at a time)
1. **Recall vs state size (discriminates 008/013/022/025 vs hybrids).** Tiny (~0.5–1M params, 2-layer, d=64) models: softmax attention, plain linear attention, gated-delta-rule recurrence (naive PyTorch loop), 3:1 linear:attention hybrid; task: MQAR (Zoology-style) with sequence length ≤128. *Pre-specified H:* gated-delta > plain linear at matched state size; 3:1 hybrid within 5 pp of attention for all numbers of KV pairs ≤ state dim. *Metric:* MQAR exact accuracy vs #pairs; 3 seeds; mean ± 95% CI. *Baseline:* attention. *Controls:* parameter-matched, label-shuffled sanity run, sequence-length held fixed. *Falsified if* hybrid is >5 pp below attention with non-overlapping CIs or delta ≈ plain linear. (Time pilot first; reduce seeds if one run >2 min.)
2. **HRM-style hierarchy vs refinement (discriminates 045/150/047).** Procedurally generated 6×6 mini-Sudoku (or 4×4), ~0.3–0.5M params; arms: (a) two-level H/L recurrence with deep supervision, (b) single-network TRM-style recursion, (c) parameter-matched one-pass Transformer, (d) Transformer with the same number of weight-tied refinement passes and deep supervision (compute-matched to a/b). *Pre-specified H:* d ≈ a within 3 pp; a, b, d > c. *Metric:* held-out exact-match vs training FLOPs, split by puzzle (no leakage), 5 seeds. *Falsified if* a exceeds d by ≥5 pp with non-overlapping CIs.
3. **Optimizer speedup vs tuning (discriminates 080 vs 081).** Tiny GPT (≈1–2M params) on a small fixed character/word corpus; AdamW vs Muon (Newton–Schulz, weight decay, update scaling as in 080) with a 5-point LR grid each, 3 seeds, 1–2k steps; control arm: AdamW at Muon's best LR (unequal-tuning artifact). *Pre-specified H:* tuned-vs-tuned Muon speedup in tokens-to-target-loss is between 1.0× and 1.5× (081-like at tiny scale) while untuned-vs-tuned shows >1.5×. *Metric:* tokens to reach a pre-set end-of-training validation loss L*, evaluated at end of schedule (not mid-training; 081 warns rankings flip). *Falsified if* tuned-vs-tuned speedup ≥1.8×.
4. **Self-correction vs equal-compute sampling (discriminates 106 vs 108/112/113).** Train a tiny Transformer on 3-digit addition/sorting; generator pass@1; arms: self-refine (model re-reads its own answer and edits it, trained with self-generated corrections), best-of-n using the model's own likelihood, best-of-n with a separately trained verifier, oracle verifier (upper bound), random pick (lower bound), majority vote at equal sample count. *Pre-specified H:* self-likelihood best-of-n and self-refine give ≤2 pp over majority-vote at equal n; a separately trained verifier gives gains proportional to its measured generation-verification gap (cf. 112). *Metric:* accuracy vs sample budget; 5 seeds. *Falsified if* self-refine beats equal-compute majority vote by ≥5 pp without an external verifier.
5. **Diffusion vs AR under repeated data (discriminates 062/063).** 1–2M-param masked-diffusion vs AR Transformer, same architecture/width, on a small corpus (≈2–5 MB text) for 1, 4, 16, 64 epochs (scale tokens per run to fit the CPU budget). *Pre-specified H:* AR wins at 1 epoch; diffusion overtakes by ≤64 epochs (crossover exists). *Metric:* held-out AR exact NLL vs diffusion ELBO (ELBO upper-bounds NLL, so diffusion < AR in ELBO is conservative evidence; any ELBO > NLL result is inconclusive). *Controls:* same optimizer/LR sweep (3 values), 3 seeds, dropout/weight-decay matched (AR may overfit by epoch count). *Falsified if* AR NLL stays below diffusion ELBO at 64 epochs with non-overlapping CIs — and then no overfitting-control explanation applies.

Suggested priority by expected information per CPU-minute: (2) and (3) (cheap, directly address the most visible contested claims), then (1), (4), (5).
